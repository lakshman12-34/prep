## How every fact was checked

Only four kinds of source are used:

| Source type | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Official** | The company's own website, job posting, annual report, results call or stock-exchange filing |
| **College notice** | A placement notice on a college's own website, or a government portal such as the apprenticeship portal |
| **News** | An established news outlet quoting a **named** company official |
| **Candidate account** | A dated, first-hand account by a student. Used **only** for test and interview details, always with the number of accounts and their dates. |

- **Not published:** the company does not publish this. We did not guess it.
- **Not verified:** only prep sites or job-listing sites claim it, so it is not used as a fact.
- Prep sites such as PrepInsta, FACE Prep, PapersAdda and the Unstop/GUVI blogs are **not used** as sources.

## What the verified research shows

1. **Companies publish very little about their tests.**
   - Only TCS, HCLTech, Accenture, L&T, Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors (Lakshya) officially publish anything about the test: sections or times.
   - **No company publishes the number of questions per section**, except Accenture's coding part (3 questions).
   - Detailed patterns such as "20 questions in 25 minutes" come only from candidates or prep sites.
2. **English communication tests are confirmed at five companies. Four of them test spoken English:**
   - HCLTech: Versant, speaking and listening, pass score 46–56 (Official).
   - Accenture: a communication section covering pronunciation, fluency and listening (Official).
   - Wipro Turbo and WILP: a voice assessment (Official, 2025 pages).
   - Cognizant: a communication test (Official). Three candidates say it includes speaking.
   - LTM: a communication section for the 2027 batch (College notice). It is not confirmed whether this is spoken.
3. **Puzzle games are reported by candidates** at Cognizant, Accenture and Capgemini. No company officially names its games.
4. **Pay is often missing from official sources:**
   - Officially published: TCS, HCLTech and L&T DET. Wipro too, but only for old batches.
   - From college notices only: Infosys SP/DSE, Cognizant (2025 batch), Capgemini and LTM.
   - Not published anywhere allowed: Accenture, Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors Lakshya. **Infosys Systems Engineer pay could not be verified.**
5. **Current marks cut-offs are official for only four companies:** TCS (60% + First Class), HCLTech (70%), L&T DET (60%) and Tata Motors Lakshya (60%).
   - Wipro's 60% is official, but only for the 2023 and 2025 batches.
   - LTM's 65% for 2027 comes from one college notice.
6. **Hiring numbers come from the companies themselves for 7 of the 12.** HCLTech's FY27 plan and Wipro's numbers come from named officials in the news. Accenture, Capgemini and Tata Electronics publish no fresher numbers. Tata Motors publishes only total programme enrolment.
7. **For diploma and ITI students the verified routes are:**
   - L&T DET: full official details.
   - Tata Motors Lakshya: Government polytechnics only.
   - Maruti Suzuki ITI Apprentice and Flexi Workmen.
   - Tata Electronics apprenticeships through the government portal.
   - Maruti's diploma JET route could not be verified this season.

## What this means for the Company Patterns page

*This section is our analysis, not company data. Nothing in the app has been changed.*

1. **Show only verified facts on each card,** with a small label showing the source type (Official, College notice, Candidate account).
2. **Say "Not published" openly** where a company doesn't publish something. Students will trust the page more than one full of guessed numbers.
3. **Keep candidate-reported patterns separate,** for example as "What students reported (3 accounts, 2025–26)", so they are never shown as official.
4. **Add a "Last checked" date to every card.** Re-check each July and again after the first drives in September–October.
5. **Practice gaps in Lyra that the verified data shows:**
   - **Spoken English:** confirmed at five companies. Scoring speech would need AI, which is outside the three agreed AI sections. A rule-based option is reading-aloud and listening practice with a checklist.
   - **Puzzle games:** candidate-reported at three companies. Simple rule-based logic puzzles would cover them.
